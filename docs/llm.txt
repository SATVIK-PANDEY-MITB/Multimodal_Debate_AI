
A Large Language Model (LLM) is a type of artificial intelligence model trained on massive text data to understand and generate human-like language.

LLMs work by predicting the next word (token) in a sequence based on context.

Tokens are small pieces of text such as words or subwords that the model processes.

Temperature controls randomness in output:
- Low temperature (0) → deterministic, factual answers
- High temperature (1) → more creative, diverse answers

Prompt engineering is the process of designing inputs to guide LLM behavior.

RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) combines LLMs with external knowledge sources to improve accuracy.

LLMs can sometimes hallucinate, meaning they generate incorrect but confident answers.

Examples of LLMs include GPT, LLaMA, and Mistral.
